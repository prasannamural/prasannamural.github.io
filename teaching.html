Philosophy

A note on teaching.

I believe materials science is best taught where the bench meets the blackboard — with hands on an electrospinning rig, eyes on an SEM, and a derivation of the Flory–Huggins parameter still on the chalk.

My courses move between theory and the lab. A lecture on polymerization kinetics lands differently after students have actually watched a PVDF solution crystallize on a collector drum, and an argument about composite micromechanics gains weight once they've broken a sample in tension. I try to teach from real data — our own group's and others' — so that the literature feels like a conversation rather than a canon.

I'm also increasingly interested in bringing machine-learning and data-driven methods into the polymer curriculum. The next generation of materials engineers will need to move comfortably between chemistry, device physics, and code — the goal is to make all three feel like one subject, not three.
